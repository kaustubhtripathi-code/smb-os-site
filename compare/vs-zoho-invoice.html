SMB OS vs Zoho Invoice

Zoho Invoice is a strong single-market tool — free, polished, deep in India. SMB OS is built for billing across markets: GST + VAT + 1099 natively, UPI/M-Pesa on the invoice, offline-first.

Zoho Invoice is genuinely good software — polished, free, and with solid GST support for India through the Zoho ecosystem. If your business lives in one country and you want the full Zoho suite behind it, it's a fine choice.

The difference is architectural: Zoho configures one home market at a time, with other geographies handled as settings. SMB OS is built market-first — India, Kenya, Nigeria, South Africa and the US each get native tax logic, local payment rails and local formats, and one account moves across all of them.

Fair is fair

When Zoho Invoice is the right choice

  • You bill in exactly one country and expect that to stay true.
  • You already run Zoho Books/CRM and want everything in one ecosystem.
  • You need full accounting (ledgers, banking) attached to invoicing today.

If that's you — genuinely, use Zoho Invoice. The moment your billing crosses a border, that's where SMB OS starts.

FAQ

The questions people actually ask.

Is Zoho Invoice free and SMB OS paid?

Zoho Invoice has a generous free plan. SMB OS is free for 10 invoices a month in one market; Pro (₹299 / $9 / KSh 500 per month) adds unlimited invoices, all markets, e-Invoice IRN and cross-device sync. If you bill in one market only, Zoho's free plan may genuinely be enough — our case is cross-border.

Does Zoho handle GST too?

Yes — Zoho's India edition has good GST support. The difference is what happens the day you also bill a US client or a Kenyan one: with SMB OS the 1099 tracking, VAT rules, M-Pesa rails and local formats are already native in the same account.

Can I migrate from Zoho Invoice?

SMB OS imports nothing automatically yet — but invoices are quick to recreate, and CSV export (QuickBooks/Xero format) means your accounting stays portable either way.
